\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

This thesis presents the design, implementation, and verification
of a J.83B Annex~B 256-QAM downstream transmit chain on the
Xilinx Zynq UltraScale+ \ac{rfsoc}~4x2 platform~\cite{AMD_UG1085}.
The transmit chain implements the full DOCSIS~3.0 downstream
physical-layer processing pipeline in custom \ac{rtl} and delivers
a 177\,MHz cable channel output at the board \ac{sma} connector.
Sections~\ref{sec:motivation} through~\ref{sec:organization} state
the motivation, problem scope, contributions, and chapter
organization.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

The Xilinx Zynq UltraScale+ \ac{rfsoc} integrates multi-gigasample
\ac{dac} and \ac{adc} converters directly on the \ac{pl} die,
making it a practical platform for cable and broadcast
physical-layer prototyping~\cite{AMD_UG1085,Kovacs2024,Javaid2022}
No open academic implementation of the J.83B Annex~B 256-QAM
downstream \ac{phy} on an \ac{rfsoc} platform has been published,
despite DOCSIS~3.0 remaining the predominant downstream physical
layer for cable modem infrastructure~\cite{ITU_J83,DOCSIS3}.
The standard MATLAB \ac{hdl} Coder toolchain path~\cite{HDLCoder}
introduced fixed-point conversion overhead and integration
constraints that made the generated \ac{rtl} unsuitable for
direct deployment, motivating a manual SystemVerilog implementation.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}

This thesis addresses the design and hardware implementation of
the J.83B Annex~B downstream transmit chain on the Zynq
UltraScale+ \ac{rfsoc}~4x2, from \ac{mpegts} input to \ac{rf}
output at a DOCSIS cable channel frequency.
The scope is restricted to the transmit direction.
The receive chain is not implemented in this work.
A secondary problem is the absence of a suitable
\ac{hdl}-Coder-generated \ac{rtl} path for J.83B Annex~B, which
motivated the manual coding approach described in
Section~\ref{sec:contributions}.

The implemented transmit chain produces a correct \ac{rf} signal
at the target frequency with verified spectral characteristics.
Consumer device lock was not achieved: two independent 256-QAM
demodulators detected the carrier but failed to acquire symbol
lock on the \ac{rfsoc} output.
The root cause was not identified within the scope of this thesis
and is treated as an open problem.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

The contributions of this thesis are:

\begin{tightemize}

  \item \textbf{Manual SystemVerilog \ac{rtl} implementation of
        the J.83B Annex~B 256-QAM downstream transmit chain}
        comprising eight custom pipeline modules, motivated by
        \ac{hdl} Coder limitations encountered during the design
        phase.

  \item \textbf{Multi-stage hardware verification methodology}
        using a stage-boundary golden vector framework for
        bit-exact simulation and \ac{dma} loopback comparison
        across all pipeline stages.

  \item \textbf{\ac{rf} diagnostic methodology for \ac{rfsoc}
        transmit systems} combining \ac{rfdc} isolation testing,
        spectral characterization, and systematic hypothesis
        elimination to localize the consumer device lock failure
        to the protocol layer.

  \item \textbf{An integrated J.83B Annex~B 256-QAM downstream
        transmit system on \ac{rfsoc}} delivered as a PYNQ
        overlay producing a continuous, gap-free output stream
        under sustained operation.

\end{tightemize}

\section{Thesis Organization}
\label{sec:organization}

Chapter~\ref{ch:fpga_background} reviews \ac{fpga} and \ac{rfsoc}
architecture, the \ac{axi} interconnect ecosystem, the \ac{rf}
clocking subsystem, the PYNQ framework, and the MATLAB-to-\ac{rtl}

algorithm background.

diagnostic results.
Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} summarizes the contributions and
identifies open problems for future work.
