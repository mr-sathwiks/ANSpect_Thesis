\chapter{DSP Algorithm Background}
\label{ch:dsp_background}

This chapter presents the signal processing background underlying
the J.83B Annex~B downstream physical layer standard, which forms
the algorithmic foundation of the transmit chain
implemented in this work.
The J.83B Annex~B standard, defined by the International
Telecommunication Union~\cite{ITU_J83} and adopted as the North
American cable modulation standard by ANSI/SCTE~\cite{SCTE07},
specifies a fixed processing chain that transforms an
\ac{mpegts} byte stream into a modulated \ac{qam256} radio-frequency signal
suitable for distribution over \ac{hfc} networks.

The processing chain, illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:j83b_chain},
consists of eight sequential stages, each treated in a dedicated
section of this chapter.
Implementation details specific to the \ac{rfsoc}~4x2 realization of
each block are deferred to Chapter~4.

\section{DOCSIS 3.0 Downstream Overview}
\label{sec:docsis_overview}

\ac{docsis} 3.0 is the cable industry standard governing broadband
data transmission over \ac{hfc} networks~\cite{DOCSIS3}.
In the downstream direction, from cable headend to \ac{cpe},
\ac{docsis}~3.0 employs \ac{scqam} channels. Each channel
occupies a 6\,MHz bandwidth slot in the North American channel
plan~\cite{SCTE07}.

The downstream channel structure relevant to this work is summarized
in Table~\ref{tab:docsis_params}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \caption{DOCSIS 3.0 downstream SC-QAM channel parameters
           (North America)~\cite{DOCSIS3,SCTE07}.}
  \label{tab:docsis_params}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \hline
    Parameter           & Value \\
    \hline
    Channel bandwidth   & 6\,MHz \\
    Modulation          & 256-QAM (J.83B Annex~B) \\
    Symbol rate         & 5.360537\,Msym/s \\
    Bits per symbol     & 8 \\
    Information bit rate & 38.81070\,Mbps \\
    FEC standard        & ITU-T J.83 Annex~B \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

A DOCSIS~3.0 downstream system bonds multiple \ac{scqam} channels to
increase aggregate throughput~\cite{DOCSIS3}.
Each individual channel is independently modulated and passes through
the complete J.83B Annex~B physical layer processing chain described
in this chapter.
The headend \ac{qam} modulator accepts a stream of \ac{mpegts}
packets from the \ac{cmts} and produces a modulated \ac{rf} signal
for distribution over the coaxial plant~\cite{DOCSIS3}.

\section{J.83B Annex B Standard}
\label{sec:j83b_standard}

ITU-T Recommendation J.83 defines digital multi-programme systems
for television, sound, and data services over cable distribution
networks~\cite{ITU_J83}.

adopted in North America, covering the downstream transmission chain
from \ac{mpegts} input to the modulated \ac{rf} output delivered
over the \ac{hfc} plant~\cite{ITU_J83,SCTE07}.
Each stage of the chain is described in

\section{Reed-Solomon Encoding}
\label{sec:rs_encoding}

\ac{rs} coding forms the outermost layer of the J.83B \ac{fec} stack,
providing block-level error correction before the data enters the
interleaver~\cite{ITU_J83}.

The J.83B \ac{rs} encoder implements a systematic, extended \ac{rs}
code; the code parameters are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:rs_params}~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The field $\mathrm{GF}(128) = \mathrm{GF}(2^7)$ is constructed
using the primitive polynomial~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  p(x) = x^7 + x^3 + 1, \quad p(\alpha) = 0
  \label{eq:rs_primitive_poly}
\end{equation}
\phantomsection\label{eq:rand_reduction_poly}% secondary — retained from §3.5
\noindent where $\alpha$ is the primitive element of $\mathrm{GF}(128)$.
The generator polynomial $g(x)$ has degree~5 with roots at $\alpha^1$
through $\alpha^5$, yielding 5 parity symbols~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \caption{J.83B Reed-Solomon code parameters~\cite{ITU_J83}.}
  \label{tab:rs_params}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \hline
    Parameter              & Value \\
    \hline
    Code                   & $(128,\,122)$ extended RS \\
    Field                  & $\mathrm{GF}(2^7) = \mathrm{GF}(128)$ \\
    Symbol width           & 7 bits \\
    Information symbols    & 122 per block \\
    Parity symbols         & 5 (RS) $+$ 1 (extended) $= 6$ total \\
    Output block size      & 128 symbols \\
    Correction capability  & $t = 3$ symbol errors per block \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The encoder multiplies the 122-symbol message polynomial by $x^5$
and divides by $g(x)$; the 5-coefficient remainder $r(x)$ forms the
RS parity symbols, which are appended to the message to yield the
systematic codeword~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  c(x) = m_{121}x^{126} + \cdots + m_0 x^5
        + r_4 x^4 + r_3 x^3 + r_2 x^2 + r_1 x + r_0
  \label{eq:rs_codeword}
\end{equation}
\noindent A valid codeword has roots at $\alpha^1$ through
$\alpha^5$~\cite{ITU_J83}.

The J.83B code is extended from $(127,\,121)$ to $(128,\,122)$
by evaluating the 127-symbol codeword at the sixth power of
$\alpha$~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  \bar{c} = c(\alpha^6)
  \label{eq:rs_extended_symbol}
\end{equation}
\noindent This extended parity symbol $\bar{c}$ is appended as the
final symbol of the transmitted RS block, yielding the complete
128-symbol output structure shown in Table~\ref{tab:rs_block}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \caption{J.83B Reed-Solomon block structure~\cite{ITU_J83}.}
  \label{tab:rs_block}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{llc}
    \hline
    Symbol positions & Content & Width \\
    \hline
    1 -- 122   & Information symbols ($m_{121}$ \ldots $m_0$) & 7 bits each \\
    123 -- 127 & RS parity symbols ($r_4$ \ldots $r_0$)       & 7 bits each \\
    128        & Extended symbol ($\bar{c} = c(\alpha^6)$)    & 7 bits \\
    \hline
    Total      & 128 symbols                                   & 896 bits \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The \ac{rs} encoder output feeds directly into the convolutional
interleaver described in Section~\ref{sec:interleaving}, which
disperses the 128-symbol \ac{rs} blocks across multiple frames to
distribute burst channel errors into isolated single-symbol
errors~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\section{Convolutional Interleaving}
\label{sec:interleaving}

Convolutional interleaving is applied to the \ac{rs}-encoded symbol
stream after the encoder and before the randomizer~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Its purpose is to disperse burst channel errors across multiple
\ac{rs} blocks so that contiguous corrupted symbols are distributed
into isolated single-symbol errors, keeping the per-block error
count within the correction capability of the $(128,\,122)$
\ac{rs} code~\cite{ITU_J83}.

The J.83B interleaver is a Forney convolutional type, operating on
7-bit symbols that match the \ac{rs} field width~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The structure consists of $I$ parallel delay branches, indexed
$b = 0, 1, \ldots, I-1$, with a commutator that cycles through
the branches sequentially at the \ac{rs} symbol rate, initialized
to branch~0 at the start of each \ac{fec} frame~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Branch $b$ introduces a delay of~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  d_b = b \cdot J \quad \text{symbols,} \quad b = 0, 1, \ldots, I-1
  \label{eq:interleaver_branch_delay}
\end{equation}
\noindent so that branch~0 passes symbols with zero delay and
branch $I-1$ introduces the maximum delay of $(I-1)\cdot J$
symbols~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The symbol latency of the interleaver is~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  L = I \cdot (I-1) \cdot J \quad \text{symbols.}
  \label{eq:interleaver_latency}
\end{equation}

%       stream and enters an input commutator on the left. The commutator

\begin{figure}[htbp]

           Branch~$b$ introduces a delay of $b \cdot J$ symbols;
           Branch~1 is a direct connection with zero delay.}
  \label{fig:interleaver}
\end{figure}

J.83B Level~2 interleaving supports \ac{qam256} modulation with
variable parameters conveyed by a 4-bit in-band control word
transmitted during the \ac{fec} frame sync interval~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Table~\ref{tab:interleaver_modes} lists the defined Level~2
parameter combinations with the corresponding burst protection and
latency figures from Table~B.2 of the standard~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \caption{J.83B Level~2 interleaver parameter options~\cite{ITU_J83}.}
  \label{tab:interleaver_modes}
  \centering

    \hline
    Control Word & $I$ & $J$ &
      Burst Protection ($\mu$s) &
      Latency, 256-QAM (ms) \\
    \hline
    0001 & 128 & 1  & 66  & 2.8  \\
    0011 &  64 & 2  & 33  & 1.4  \\
    0101 &  32 & 4  & 16  & 0.68 \\
    0111 &  16 & 8  & 8.2 & 0.33 \\

    \hline

\end{table}

This implementation uses control word \texttt{0001}, corresponding
to $I = 128$ branches and $J = 1$, the maximum-depth Level~2
configuration~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Substituting into~\eqref{eq:interleaver_latency},
\begin{equation}
  L = 128 \cdot 127 \cdot 1 = 16{,}256 \quad \text{symbols,}
  \label{eq:interleaver_latency_val}
\end{equation}
\noindent corresponding to a one-way \ac{qam256} channel latency
of 2.8\,ms and burst protection of $66\,\mu\mathrm{s}$, as
specified in Table~B.2 of the standard~\cite{ITU_J83}.

The interleaved symbol stream feeds directly into the randomizer
described in Section~\ref{sec:randomization}, where a \ac{pn}
sequence is applied for spectral whitening~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\section{Randomization}
\label{sec:randomization}

The randomizer is applied to the interleaved symbol stream after
the convolutional interleaver and before frame sync
encoding~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Its function is to ensure even distribution of symbols across the
\ac{qam256} constellation; without randomization, long runs of
identical symbols produce spectral lines in the transmitted
signal~\cite{ITU_J83}.

The randomizer XORs each 7-bit interleaved symbol with a
corresponding symbol from a \ac{pn} sequence defined over
$\mathrm{GF}(128) = \mathrm{GF}(2^7)$~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The \ac{pn} sequence is generated by a \ac{lfsr} governed by
the GF(128) recurrence polynomial~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  f(x) = x^3 + x + \alpha^3
  \label{eq:rand_poly}
\end{equation}
\noindent where $\alpha$ is the primitive element of
$\mathrm{GF}(128)$, satisfying the same reduction polynomial as
the \ac{rs} encoder~\eqref{eq:rs_primitive_poly}~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The \ac{lfsr} consists of three 7-bit state registers; at each
clock step the state advances according to~\eqref{eq:rand_poly},
producing one 7-bit \ac{pn} output symbol per step~\cite{ITU_J83}.

The randomizer is re-initialized at each \ac{fec} frame boundary
by loading all three state registers to \texttt{0x7F}~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Initialization occurs during the \ac{fec} frame sync trailer
interval; the trailer symbols themselves are therefore not
randomized~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The randomizer parameters for \ac{qam256} operation are summarized
in Table~\ref{tab:rand_params}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \caption{J.83B Annex~B randomizer parameters for
           256-QAM operation~\cite{ITU_J83}.}
  \label{tab:rand_params}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \hline
    Parameter              & Value \\
    \hline
    Sequence type          & \ac{lfsr} over $\mathrm{GF}(2^7)$ \\
    Recurrence polynomial  & $f(x) = x^3 + x + \alpha^3$ \\
    Reduction polynomial   & $p(x) = x^7 + x^3 + 1$ \\
    Symbol width           & 7 bits \\
    Initialization state   & \texttt{0x7F} (all ones) \\
    Init trigger           & \ac{fec} frame trailer interval \\
    Operation              & Bitwise XOR per symbol \\
    Sequence period        & $88 \times 128 = 11{,}264$ symbols \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The randomized symbol stream feeds directly into the frame sync
encoder described in Section~\ref{sec:framesync}, where the
\ac{fec} frame trailer is appended and the symbol stream is
serialized into a bit stream for trellis coding~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\section{Frame Sync Encoding}
\label{sec:framesync}

Frame sync encoding appends a fixed-pattern trailer to the
randomized symbol stream at the end of each \ac{fec}
frame~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The trailer delineates the frame boundary and carries the 4-bit
in-band control word for the active interleaver depth
mode~\cite{ITU_J83}.
For \ac{qam256}, trellis group boundaries are additionally aligned
to the \ac{fec} frame by the frame sync encoder~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\paragraph{FEC frame structure:}
For \ac{qam256} operation, one \ac{fec} frame consists of 88
\ac{rs} blocks each containing 128 seven-bit symbols~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Each 7-bit symbol is serialized \ac{msb}-first into a contiguous
bit stream, producing~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  N_\text{data} = 88 \times 128 \times 7 = 78{,}848 \text{ bits}
  \label{eq:fec_data_bits}
\end{equation}
\noindent of data per frame.
The 40-bit sync trailer is appended at the end of the data bit
stream, giving a total frame length of~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  N_\text{frame} = 78{,}848 + 40 = 78{,}888 \text{ bits}
  \label{eq:fec_frame_bits}
\end{equation}
\noindent which divides exactly into $2{,}076 \times 38$ bits,
corresponding to 2{,}076 trellis groups of 38 input bits
each~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The \ac{rs} block and 7-bit symbol boundaries are aligned to the
end of the \ac{fec} frame~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \caption{J.83B Annex~B 256-QAM \ac{fec} frame
           arithmetic~\cite{ITU_J83}.}
  \label{tab:fec_frame_arith}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lr}
    \hline
    Quantity & Value \\
    \hline
    RS blocks per frame           & 88 \\
    Symbols per RS block          & 128 \\
    Bits per symbol (serialized)  & 7 \\
    Data bits per frame           & 78{,}848 \\
    Sync trailer bits             & 40 \\
    Total bits per frame          & 78{,}888 \\
    Trellis groups per frame      & 2{,}076 \\
    Bits per trellis group        & 38 \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Sync trailer format:}
The 40-bit sync trailer is structured as three fields packed
\ac{msb}-first, as shown in
Table~\ref{tab:framesync_trailer}~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \caption{J.83B Annex~B 256-QAM \ac{fec} frame sync trailer
           structure~\cite{ITU_J83}.}
  \label{tab:framesync_trailer}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{clcc}
    \hline
    Field & Content & Width & Value \\
    \hline
    Unique word  & Fixed synchronization pattern & 32 bits
                 & \texttt{71 E8 4D D4}\textsubscript{HEX} \\
    Control word & Interleaver depth mode        & 4 bits
                 & Level~2 mode code \\
    Reserved     & Set to zero                   & 4 bits
                 & \texttt{0x0} \\
    \hline
    Total        & Sync trailer                  & 40 bits & --- \\
    \hline
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The sync trailer is inserted by the encoder and is not
randomized~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The randomizer is initialized during the trailer interval so that
its output is ready to scramble the first data symbol of the
following frame~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\paragraph{Symbol serialization:}
The frame sync encoder serializes each 7-bit \ac{rs} symbol into
individual bits, emitting the \ac{msb} first~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The \ac{msb} of the first \ac{rs} symbol of each \ac{fec} frame is
assigned to the first bit position of the first non-sync trellis
group~\cite{ITU_J83}.

The serialized bit stream, comprising the $78{,}848$ data bits and
the 40-bit trailer (Table~\ref{tab:fec_frame_arith}), feeds
directly into the trellis encoder described in
Section~\ref{sec:trellis}~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\section{Trellis Coding}
\label{sec:trellis}

Trellis coding forms the inner code of the J.83B concatenated
coding scheme~\cite{ITU_J83}.
It introduces redundancy into the transmitted symbol stream to
improve the threshold \ac{snr} by expanding the symbol
constellation without increasing the symbol rate, and is more
precisely termed trellis coded modulation~\cite{ITU_J83}.
For \ac{qam256}, the trellis encoder accepts the serialized bit
stream from the frame sync encoder and produces 8-bit
trellis-coded words (C-words) that address the \ac{qam256}
constellation mapper~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\paragraph{Trellis group structure:}
The trellis encoder operates on fixed-length trellis
groups~\cite{ITU_J83}.
For \ac{qam256}, each group contains 38 input bits and produces
5 output symbols (40 bits); non-sync groups source all 38 bits
from the serialized \ac{rs} stream, while sync groups replace
8 bits with sync bits drawn from the \ac{fec} frame
trailer~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The 78{,}888-bit frame (Section~\ref{sec:framesync}) divides
into 2{,}076 trellis groups: 2{,}071 non-sync and 5 sync groups
at the end of the frame~\cite{ITU_J83}.

Within each group, the 38 input bits are partitioned by a data
formatter into four streams: the \ac{msb}s and \ac{lsb}s of the
`A' symbols, and the \ac{msb}s and \ac{lsb}s of the `B'
symbols~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The \ac{msb} streams bypass the convolutional encoder and pass
directly to the \ac{qam256} mapper uncoded; the \ac{lsb} streams
are differentially precoded and convolutionally encoded before
mapping~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\paragraph{Binary convolutional coder:}
The convolutional encoder is a 16-state, non-systematic,
rate~$\frac{1}{2}$ \ac{bcc} with generator
polynomials~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{align}
  G_1(D) &= 1 + D^2 + D^4
  \label{eq:bcc_g1} \\
  G_2(D) &= 1 + D + D^2 + D^3 + D^4
  \label{eq:bcc_g2}
\end{align}
\noindent corresponding to the octal pair
$(G_1,\,G_2) = (25_8,\,37_8)$~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The commutator is initialized to the $G_1$ position at the start
of each trellis group~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Four input bits per group produce 8 encoded bits, reduced to 5
by the puncture matrix~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} P_1 \\ P_2 \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
  \label{eq:bcc_puncture}
\end{equation}
\noindent where $1$ denotes transmission and $0$ denotes
suppression, giving a punctured rate of $\frac{4}{5}$~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Two independent \ac{bcc} instances operate in parallel: one for
the `A' \ac{lsb} stream (outputs $U_1$--$U_5$) and one for the
`B' \ac{lsb} stream (outputs $V_1$--$V_5$)~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\paragraph{Differential precoder:}
The \ac{lsb} streams pass through a differential precoder that
provides 90° rotational invariance~\cite{ITU_J83}.
For \ac{qam256}, bits $C_4$ and $C_0$ of each C-word are
differentially encoded per the precoder update
equations~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{align}
  x_J &= W_J \oplus x_{J-1} \oplus Z_J \cdot
         (x_{J-1} \oplus Y_{J-1})
  \label{eq:precode_x} \\
  Y_J &= Z_J \oplus W_J \oplus Y_{J-1} \oplus Z_J \cdot
         (x_{J-1} \oplus Y_{J-1})
  \label{eq:precode_y}
\end{align}
\noindent where $W_J$ and $Z_J$ are the two input bits at step
$J$, $x_J$ and $Y_J$ are the precoded output bits, $\oplus$
denotes XOR, and $(\cdot)$ denotes AND~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\paragraph{Code rate and output:}
Each trellis group produces 40 output bits from 38 input bits,
giving an overall \ac{tcm} rate of~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  R_\text{TCM} = \frac{38}{40} = \frac{19}{20}
  \label{eq:trellis_rate}
\end{equation}
\noindent The 40 output bits are packed into 5 eight-bit C-words,

\section{256-QAM Symbol Mapping}
\label{sec:qam256}

The \ac{qam256} symbol mapper converts the 8-bit C-words
produced by the trellis encoder into complex \ac{iq} baseband
symbols for transmission~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Each C-word addresses a look-up table that returns the complex
symbol coordinates corresponding to that C-word's position on the
\ac{qam256} constellation defined by Figure~B.19 of the
standard~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The mapper produces one complex sample per C-word, forming a
stream at the J.83B symbol rate of 5.360537\,Msym/s that feeds
directly into the \ac{srrc} pulse shaping filter described in
Section~\ref{sec:srrc}~\cite{ITU_J83}.

The J.83B constellation is a square $16 \times 16$ grid in the
\ac{iq} plane, with 256 distinct symbol positions and minimum
Euclidean distance uniform across the grid~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The trellis encoder delivers the coded and uncoded 4-bit streams
to the mapper, which concatenates them into a single 8-bit C-word
with ordering $C_7 C_6 C_5 C_4\,C_3 C_2 C_1 C_0$ where $C_7$
is the \ac{msb}; the mapping is a bijection over the range
$0$\,--\,$255$~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The differential precoder bits $C_4$ and $C_0$ occupy the two
differentially encoded positions within the C-word, as noted in
Section~\ref{sec:trellis}~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The mapper output is a stream of complex baseband samples
$s_k = I_k + j Q_k$, where $I_k$ and $Q_k$ are the in-phase
and quadrature coordinates of the $k$-th mapped
symbol~\cite{ITU_J83}.

\section{SRRC Pulse Shaping}
\label{sec:srrc}

\ac{srrc} pulse shaping is the final stage of the J.83B transmit
processing chain, applied to the complex \ac{iq} symbol stream
after \ac{qam256} mapping~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The \ac{srrc} filter bandlimits the symbol stream to fit within
the 6\,MHz channel allocation and controls the spectral shape of
the transmitted signal~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The standard refers to this filter as a square-root raised cosine
filter in Table~B.3 and as a Root-Nyquist filter in
\S B.6.2~\cite{ITU_J83}.
The cascade of the transmit and receive \ac{srrc} filters
satisfies the Nyquist \ac{isi} criterion~\cite{proakis_dsp},
ensuring zero \ac{isi} at the receiver sampling instants when
the channel is ideal.

\paragraph{J.83B filter specification:}
For \ac{qam256}, Table~B.3 of the standard specifies the transmit
filter as a square-root raised cosine with roll-off factor
$\alpha = 0.12$ and symbol rate
$f_\text{sym} = 5.360537\,\text{Msym/s} \pm 5\,\text{ppm}$~\cite{ITU_J83}.
Substituting into the bandwidth expression, the double-sided
occupied bandwidth of the J.83B \ac{qam256} channel
is~\cite{ITU_J83}
\begin{equation}
  B = (1 + \alpha) \cdot f_\text{sym}
    = 1.12 \times 5.360537 \approx 6.004\,\text{MHz}
  \label{eq:srrc_bw}
\end{equation}
\noindent which fits within the 6\,MHz channel slot defined by the
North American channel plan~\cite{ITU_J83,SCTE07}.
